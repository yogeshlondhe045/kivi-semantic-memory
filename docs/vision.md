# Product vision

## What I noticed

Dictating for a week, the thing I kept wanting was never "know me better". It was narrower and duller: stop making me repeat myself. Names the recogniser mangles. The way I write in Slack versus email. The message I dictated on Thursday that I now need again on Monday.

Memory features in other products mostly promise the opposite. They offer a system that accumulates a picture of you, shown back as a tidy list of traits with no way to check where any line came from. The relationship being proposed is intimacy. What is actually delivered is an unverifiable profile you are invited to trust.

Speech makes that trade worse. Typing is edited; speech is not. People dictate while walking out of a difficult meeting, while irritated, while talking about a colleague who is unwell. A system that treats everything it hears as evidence about a person will learn things nobody chose to tell it. The microphone is the reason to be more careful here, not less.

## The position

Kivi's memory should be a ledger, not a portrait. It records claims the person actually made, keeps the sentence that supports each one, and can produce that sentence on demand. It never infers a trait from a pattern.

This is a smaller promise than the industry standard, and a much easier one to keep.

## Where the value is

Three things, in order of how often they matter.

Kivi should stop getting your vocabulary wrong. Names, projects, tools — learned from how you have used them, applied while you dictate. This is the only place memory touches ordinary dictation, and it is the only place it should: correcting a spelling is verifiable and reversible, while rewriting meaning is neither.

Kivi should let you act on your own history by describing it the way you remember it — "the one from around five yesterday, in Slack" — and then do something useful with what it finds. Retrieving the right recording is most of that job. Rewriting it, using how you have said you write, is the rest.

Kivi should answer questions about what you have said, with sources. Who owns this now. What did I promise, and when. Not a summary of you: a citation from you.

## What deserves to be remembered

Things you stated outright, and things you repeated. Repetition is the strongest available evidence that something is durable, and it costs nothing to wait for. Names, roles, ownership, stated preferences, promises with deadlines — each with the dictation it came from.

Everything else is kept as a recording and nothing more. Most of what anyone says is not worth remembering, and a memory that keeps it all is a search index wearing a memory's clothes.

## What Kivi must never assume

That a pattern is a trait. That a passing state is a standing fact. That hearing something makes it Kivi's to know — health, money, politics, faith, relationships, another person's private circumstances are never recorded, whatever is said. That silence is consent: a low-confidence belief waits to be confirmed instead of being used quietly. That correcting itself is optional.

And it must never fill a gap. An invented answer, delivered fluently, destroys the value of every true one.

## Why someone keeps using it

Because everything it claims can be checked in one click, and because it says "I don't have that" often enough to be believed when it says anything else.

This rules out things I would enjoy building: mood detection, predictive suggestion, a personality model that gets uncannily good. Those make a better demo. They make a worse thing to speak into every day.
